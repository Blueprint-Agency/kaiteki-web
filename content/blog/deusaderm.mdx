## Introduction

[Deusaderm](/technology/deusaderm) is an injectable collagen gel made by Sunmax Biotechnology in Taiwan. It is cross-linked type I collagen purified from pig (porcine) skin, it contains no hyaluronic acid, and the version supplied in Malaysia also contains lidocaine, a local anaesthetic. At Kaiteki it is used within our [bio-stimulator treatment](/treatments/bio-stimulator). This post looks at why doctors consider it where hyaluronic acid filler has left a face looking overfilled, and what its limits are.

## The Downside of Hyaluronic Acid Fillers

We are familiar with Hyaluronic Acid based fillers. They give some change straight away, but in some people, particularly under the eyes where fluid tends to collect, they can contribute to swelling and water retention.

Hyaluronic acid has a strong affinity for water molecules, which makes it perfect for maintaining moisture and hydration.

However, when it is used in areas with poor lymphatic drainage, it can result in fluid retention and puffiness, leading to an overfilled appearance.

## What is Deusaderm?

Here’s why doctors consider Deusaderm as an alternative in some areas, especially the under-eye region and overfilled faces.

Deusaderm is an injectable collagen gel developed by Sunmax Biotechnology in Taiwan. The manufacturer lists it as cross-linked type I atelocollagen purified from the skin of specific-pathogen-free pigs. Atelocollagen is collagen with the end segments of the molecule removed, the parts most associated with allergic reactions. Because it is animal-derived, your doctor will still take an allergy history, and if a porcine product is not acceptable to you for religious, dietary or personal reasons, non-animal options can be discussed instead.

Sunmax’s first injectable collagen received a Taiwan Food and Drug Administration licence in 2006. In Malaysia, Deusaderm is regulated as a medical device.

## Water, Puffiness and the Tyndall Effect

Because it does not contain hyaluronic acid, a water-drawing (hydrophilic) compound, it does not bind water the way an HA filler does, which is why doctors consider it where puffiness or an overfilled look is the concern. It is not swelling-free, though: swelling, redness, tenderness and bruising are common in the first days, and they tend to show more under the eyes.

It is also white and opaque rather than clear, so it does not show the bluish tint (Tyndall effect) that a clear gel placed too close to the surface can cause. The Tyndall effect was not observed in a 2024 trial of Sunmax’s collagen.

## How Does Deusaderm Differ from Traditional HA Fillers?

Traditional HA fillers like Juvederm, Restylane fillers are gel based injectables. They primarily work as a space filler, giving volume replacement with minimal stimulation of new collagen.

On the other hand, a collagen-based injectable like Deusaderm places purified collagen itself, giving some support when injected before it is absorbed over the following months. The manufacturer also describes it as supporting the skin’s own collagen over time, but the published studies measured wrinkle grading and skin readings rather than new collagen formation directly, so treat that as the manufacturer’s account rather than a proven effect.

## Is Deusaderm the Future of Injectables?

It is a useful option in the right case rather than a replacement. HA fillers remain the usual choice for contour and volume, and they have one safety advantage collagen lacks: they can be dissolved. Deusaderm suits a narrower set of concerns, such as hollowness under the eyes or an overfilled look after repeated HA filler, where its lack of water-binding and its opaque colour matter. Any change is temporary, because the collagen is absorbed over months, and results vary between individuals.

As with any injectable, it carries risks, which are explained during consultation. In the 2024 trial the effects recorded were mostly at the injection site, including swelling, pain, bruising, small lumps (nodules), redness and itching. It is not used in pregnancy or while breastfeeding, and tell your doctor about any allergy to collagen, pork-derived products or lidocaine.
