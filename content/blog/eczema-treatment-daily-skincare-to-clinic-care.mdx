## What is eczema?

Eczema is a chronic skin condition caused by a combination of an overactive immune system and a weakened skin barrier. When something irritates or triggers the immune response, inflammation beneath the skin's surface leads to persistent dryness, redness, itching, and sometimes cracking or weeping skin.

The condition is not contagious and cannot be passed through physical contact. It is, however, strongly genetic. If one or both parents have eczema, asthma, or hay fever, the risk increases significantly. While many children outgrow it, eczema can persist into adulthood or appear for the first time in adults who have never dealt with it before.

### Common types of eczema

Atopic dermatitis is the most widespread form, causing dry, itchy patches that favour the inner elbows, behind the knees, and the face. Contact dermatitis develops when the skin reacts to a specific irritant like detergent, fragrance, or nickel. Dyshidrotic eczema produces small, fluid filled blisters on the fingers and palms. Hand eczema is common among people who wash their hands frequently or work with chemicals. Nummular eczema shows up as round, coin shaped patches of irritated skin.

## What triggers eczema flare ups?

Flare ups are almost always triggered by something identifiable. In Malaysia, the constant cycling between hot, humid outdoor air and dry, air conditioned indoor spaces creates repeated moisture fluctuations that the skin barrier struggles to handle. Dust mites, which thrive in warm, humid environments, are a major trigger for many people.

Irritants in everyday products, including soaps, detergents, and skincare containing sodium lauryl sulfate (SLS), fragrances, or alcohol, can aggravate sensitive skin. Allergens like pollen, pet dander, and certain foods (dairy, eggs, nuts, wheat) can also worsen flares. Stress and hormonal changes during menstruation, pregnancy, or menopause are well documented triggers.

## Building a daily skincare routine for eczema

The foundation of effective eczema treatment is a consistent daily routine. Use a fragrance free, soap free cleanser and keep baths short (10 to 15 minutes) with lukewarm water. Within 3 minutes of bathing, apply a thick moisturiser to slightly damp skin to lock in hydration.

Look for moisturisers containing ceramides (which rebuild the skin barrier), colloidal oatmeal (which soothes inflammation), or hyaluronic acid. Ointments and thick creams work better than lotions. Our [skincare range](/products) includes ceramide based options formulated for sensitive, dehydrated skin. For mild flares, OTC hydrocortisone cream (0.5% to 1%) can reduce redness and itching when applied only to affected areas.

Avoid products with SLS, fragrances (synthetic or natural), denatured alcohol, essential oils, and artificial dyes. If a product makes your skin sting or tingle, it is likely irritating your barrier.

### The gut health and eczema connection

Growing evidence links gut microbiome imbalance to chronic inflammatory skin conditions including eczema. Probiotic rich foods (yogurt, kefir, kimchi, tempeh) can support a healthier gut environment. Anti inflammatory foods like omega 3 rich fish, leafy greens, berries, and turmeric may help reduce overall inflammation.

Common dietary triggers include dairy, gluten, eggs, soy, and highly processed foods. A food and symptom diary can help identify personal patterns. Consult a doctor before eliminating entire food groups, especially for children.

## When daily skincare is not enough

A doctor's evaluation is needed if flare ups persist after two weeks of consistent OTC treatment, itching regularly disrupts sleep, or signs of skin infection appear (oozing, yellow crusting, increased redness, swelling, fever). Rapidly spreading patches, thickened or leathery skin from chronic scratching, or [acne](/concerns/acne) developing alongside eczema are also signals that professional support is needed.

## Professional and clinical eczema treatments

Doctors follow a stepped approach, starting with the mildest effective option.

### Prescription topical corticosteroids

Stronger than OTC hydrocortisone, prescribed at a potency matched to severity and body area. Milder formulations for the face, stronger ones for the body. Safe and effective when used under medical guidance.

### Topical calcineurin inhibitors

Steroid free alternatives like tacrolimus and pimecrolimus for sensitive areas (face, neck, groin). They suppress the local immune response without the skin thinning risk of prolonged steroid use.

### Oral medications

Sedating antihistamines help with nighttime itching. For severe cases, oral immunosuppressants like cyclosporine calm the overactive immune system but require regular monitoring due to potential side effects.

### Phototherapy

Controlled doses of ultraviolet B (UVB) light slow the skin's inflammatory response. Sessions are done two to three times per week, with improvements typically visible within a few weeks.

### Biologics

Dupilumab (Dupixent) targets specific immune pathways driving eczema inflammation. A pivotal trial published in the *New England Journal of Medicine* showed up to 75% skin clearance in moderate to severe cases. Reserved for persistent eczema unresponsive to other treatments.

## Other skin conditions you should know about

### Warts and warts removal cream

Warts are caused by HPV and people with weakened skin barriers may be more susceptible. OTC warts removal cream products containing salicylic acid dissolve wart layers over several weeks. For stubborn warts, professional cryotherapy or laser removal is more effective.

### Keloid scars and keloid removal cream

Chronic eczema scratching can damage skin and lead to keloid formation in genetically predisposed individuals. Silicone based keloid removal cream products help flatten and soften scars over time. For larger keloids, corticosteroid injections or laser therapy deliver better results. If you notice unusual skin bumps or growing raised scars, consult a doctor.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is eczema curable?

There is no permanent cure, but eczema can be managed effectively with consistent skincare and medical treatment when needed. Many children outgrow it, and adults can achieve long periods of clear skin.

### Can eczema spread to other people?

No. Eczema is not contagious. It is an immune mediated condition driven by genetics and environmental triggers.

### Is steroid cream safe for long term use?

Yes, when used correctly under medical guidance. Doctors match potency to the body area and may alternate with steroid free options like calcineurin inhibitors for ongoing management.

### What foods trigger eczema?

Common triggers include dairy, eggs, nuts, wheat, soy, and processed foods. Triggers vary by individual. A food diary helps identify patterns. Consult a professional before eliminating food groups.

### Can eczema leave permanent scars?

Eczema itself rarely scars, but persistent scratching can cause thickened, darkened, or lightened skin patches. Deep scratching wounds may heal with scarring, especially in people prone to keloids.
