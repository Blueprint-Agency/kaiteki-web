XERF, Oligio X and Density are all radiofrequency (RF) devices that warm the deeper layers of the skin from the surface, with no needles. They are aimed at the same problem: early to moderate loss of firmness, mostly on the lower face, jawline and neck. Where they differ is in how the energy goes in, how the skin surface is protected, and what each has been studied on.

Patients often come in with screenshots from Korean clinics and ask me which one is "the strongest". That question does not get you very far. A more useful one is: is loose skin really my problem, and if so, which way of heating suits my face? This guide explains the three devices in plain terms, what the published studies do and do not show, and what a doctor weighs before choosing one, or none.

## What the three have in common

All three are monopolar RF devices at heart. Current passes from the treatment tip into the skin, meets the tissue's natural resistance and turns into heat in the dermis. That warmth makes existing collagen contract a little, then prompts slower collagen remodelling over the following months. This is why any firming from RF builds gradually rather than showing on the day.

All three also cool the skin surface while the heat builds underneath. Cooling is not a comfort extra. It is what lets the dermis reach a useful temperature without the top layer of skin becoming the limit.

And all three work over intact skin. There are no channels, no pinpoint marks, and most people go back to work the same day. ## How each one delivers the heat

### Oligio X

[Oligio X](/technology/oligio) is made by Wontech in South Korea. It runs at a single frequency, 6.78 MHz, and is delivered shot by shot through a disposable tip. The tip cools the skin on contact and monitors contact and tissue impedance, which helps the energy go in evenly from one shot to the next.

In practice it is a straightforward, well-understood design. Numbing cream can be applied first if you are worried about comfort, and the doctor can adjust the energy during the session.

### XERF

[XERF](/technology/xerf) is made by Cynosure Lutronic. Its point of difference is that it is multifrequency: it runs 6.78 MHz and 2 MHz together. Higher frequencies deposit heat more superficially and lower frequencies travel further, so combining the two is meant to spread heat across shallower and deeper layers in one pass, instead of settling for one depth.

Cooling comes from an integrated cryogen system, and the manufacturer markets XERF as needing no numbing cream. I will come back to that claim below, because the published data on comfort is more mixed than the marketing.

### Density

[Density](/technology/density) is made by Jeisys Medical, the Korean company that also makes the [Potenza](/technology/potenza) RF microneedling platform. Density itself has no needles. With its dual-mode tips, each shot delivers monopolar RF and then bipolar RF in sequence through the same tip, also at 6.78 MHz, while cryogen cooling protects the surface. The monopolar phase reaches deeper; the bipolar phase, where current runs between closely spaced electrodes, stays in the upper dermis. The idea is to treat two depths within a single shot.

You may see it called Density Noir. That is the current version of the system, and it is the name used in the most recent studies.

## What the published studies actually show

This is the part most comparison pages skip, so I will be direct about it. All three devices have some published clinical data, and all of it is small.

- **Oligio.** A 2022 study in *Dermatology and Therapy* treated 30 Thai patients with skin types III to V and mild to moderate lower-face laxity with one session, and blinded dermatologists graded improvement over six months, with no serious side effects reported. A 2024 Korean study of 20 women reported improvement in jawline and nasolabial sagging that held to 24 weeks. Both looked at the original Oligio, which Oligio X builds on.
- **XERF.** A 2026 retrospective study in *Lasers in Medical Science* reviewed 16 women after a single session, using 3D imaging and questionnaires to three months. The authors are clear that it had no control group and no comparison with single-frequency devices, so it cannot say whether two frequencies do better than one. A separate 2026 paper in *Cureus* introduced its smaller tips for the eye area.
- **Density.** A 2026 open-label study in the *Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology* treated 26 Thai adults aged 30 to 60 with mild to moderate lower-face laxity, with one session and follow-up to six months. Laxity ratings improved, and the side effects were temporary redness, swelling and mild pain. One author works for the manufacturer, and the authors call for controlled studies. A three-patient case series in *Clinical, Cosmetic and Investigational Dermatology* looked at the eye tip.

Two things stand out. First, every study is on mild to moderate laxity, which tells you who these devices are for. Second, none of them compares XERF, Oligio and Density with each other. A clinic that tells you one is clearly superior is going beyond the evidence.

## How a doctor chooses between them

When I see a patient asking about these three, the device is almost the last decision. The order of questions matters more.

**1. Is it loose skin?** A softer jawline can come from loose skin, but also from fat under the chin, volume loss in the cheeks, a short chin or simply bone structure. RF only addresses the first. If fat or volume is the main issue, [HIFU](/treatments/hifu), fillers or a fat treatment may fit better, and our page on [face contouring](/concerns/face-contouring) explains how those are told apart.

**2. How much laxity?** Early to moderate laxity is where all three were studied. Marked sagging, heavy jowls or a lot of loose neck skin are generally beyond what any surface RF device can do, and I would rather say so at the consultation than sell a session.

**3. Which area?** The full face and jawline can be treated with any of the three. Around the eyes, each has a dedicated small tip, so the choice comes down to the eye protocol and the doctor's experience with it, not the brand on the machine.

**4. How do you handle heat?** Some patients tolerate deep warmth easily. Others find it uncomfortable and do better with numbing cream and lower, more gradual settings. This is a fair reason to prefer one platform over another, and your history with past RF or HIFU sessions helps here.

**5. What else is in your plan?** RF is often one part of a plan rather than the whole of it. It can sit alongside injectables or other energy devices, timed so one does not interfere with another. Recent fillers, threads, retinoids or peels all affect timing and settings, so mention them.

Only after those five questions does it make sense to pick a device. At that point the differences between the three are real but modest, and the person operating the machine matters at least as much as the machine.

## Is one better for Malaysian skin?

Not in a way that should decide your choice. RF heats tissue rather than targeting pigment, so it does not depend on skin colour the way some lasers do. The Oligio and Density studies were both done in Thai patients, which is helpful for our skin types.

That does not mean zero risk. Too much heat in one spot can still cause blistering, a burn or a change in pigmentation, which is why cooling, energy settings and a trained operator matter. Afterwards, our heat and humidity make proper sun protection more important, not less. Redness usually settles within hours, but avoid hot yoga, saunas and long midday sun for a day or two.

Like other RF devices, none of the three is used during pregnancy, with a pacemaker or other implanted electronic device, over active infection or inflamed skin, or with a history of keloid scarring. Your doctor will go through the full list with you.
