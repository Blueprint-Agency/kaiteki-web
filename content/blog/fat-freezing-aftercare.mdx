Fat freezing (cryolipolysis) cools a pocket of fat through the skin, so the treated area reacts for a while afterwards. Redness, numbness, firmness and some tenderness are expected and usually settle on their own. A small number of effects are not part of normal recovery and should be shown to a doctor.

This guide walks through what is normal after a session, what helps, the rare side effect worth knowing by name, and when to call the clinic.

## What is normal after fat freezing?

Straight after the applicator comes off, the area often looks red and feels firm, cold or oddly numb. This typically settles over the following hours to days.

Over the first weeks, temporary tenderness, swelling, tingling, itching, bruising or a dull ache can come and go in the treated area. In the case series that first described its rare complication, published in *JAMA Dermatology*, the authors also noted that a temporary decrease in sensation was common and could last up to about eight weeks.

### Pain that starts a few days later

Some people feel little at first, then notice a deeper ache, tingling or sensitivity a few days after the session. Delayed-onset pain after cryolipolysis is described in *Dermatologic Surgery* as usually settling on its own. It is still worth telling the clinic about, because your doctor can advise on what is safe to take and rule out anything else.

There is no special diet to follow. Any change in the area is gradual, because the body clears the treated fat cells over several weeks, so it is usually reviewed with your doctor a couple of months after the session rather than judged in the first days.

## The rare side effect worth knowing: paradoxical adipose hyperplasia

Paradoxical adipose hyperplasia (PAH) is a delayed effect in which the treated pocket of fat gradually becomes firmer and larger instead of reducing. It usually shows up weeks to months after treatment, often in the shape of the applicator. It was first reported in *JAMA Dermatology* in 2014 as rare.

Later research suggests it is more common than the manufacturer's early figures. A 2025 systematic review in *Aesthetic Surgery Journal Open Forum*, pooling 28 studies and about 13,000 patients, estimated roughly 1 case in every 455 patients, while noting the evidence is of low certainty. Reports have described it as relatively more frequent in men.

PAH does not go away on its own and is not treated with more fat freezing. If you notice a treated area becoming firmer or larger rather than smaller, see your doctor so it can be assessed and the options discussed.

## When to contact the clinic

<Note icon="shield" title="Contact the clinic promptly if" body="Pain is severe or keeps getting worse, the skin blisters, breaks, darkens or turns pale and stays that way, you notice a lump or swelling that grows, or a treated area becomes firmer and larger over the following weeks or months." />

These are uncommon, but they are not part of normal recovery and should be checked rather than waited out.

## Who should not have fat freezing at all

Fat freezing is not a weight-loss treatment, and not every area can be held by an applicator. It is deferred in pregnancy and breastfeeding, and avoided with cold-related conditions such as cold urticaria or cryoglobulinaemia, a hernia near the area, or broken skin over it.
