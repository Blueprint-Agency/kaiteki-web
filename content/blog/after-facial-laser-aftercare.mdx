After a facial laser, your skin is recovering from the energy it has just absorbed. For a while it is redder, more sensitive and, in the medium to deeper skin tones common in Malaysia, more prone to darkening. Good aftercare lets it settle quietly. Rules with no basis just make the week harder.

This guide separates the two: the restrictions with a medical reason behind them, and the ones handed down that do not hold up.

## Why skin needs care after laser

A laser delivers energy into the skin, and the skin answers with a short burst of inflammation. Inflammation can prompt pigment cells to make more melanin, which shows as darkening called post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH).

A review in the *Journal of Clinical and Aesthetic Dermatology* describes PIH as more frequent and longer-lasting in skin of colour, including Asian skin, with sun protection a core part of preventing it. Mayo Clinic's guidance on laser resurfacing also stresses sun protection afterwards because of the risk of colour change. Most of the advice below comes down to one idea: do not add more inflammation while the skin is still healing.

## What to avoid, and why

### Sun and strong light

Use a broad-spectrum sunscreen every day, indoors near windows too, and reapply when outside. Add a hat or umbrella and avoid the midday sun. This is the most important rule, and the one to keep longest.

### Heat and heavy sweating

Saunas, steam rooms, hot yoga and hard workouts raise skin temperature and can prolong redness. Avoid them for the period your doctor specifies, usually the first few days.

### Active skincare and scrubs

Pause retinoids (including tretinoin and retinol), AHAs, BHAs, high-strength vitamin C and physical scrubs. Use a gentle cleanser and a plain moisturiser, and restart actives only when your doctor clears it.

### Picking crusts or peeling skin

After some pigment lasers, fine dots like coffee grounds appear and flake off by themselves. After stronger lasers, the skin may peel. Do not pick, rub or pull at either.

### Makeup, pools and other treatments

Makeup can go back on once redness settles, over intact skin only. Avoid chlorinated pools, waxing, threading and other facial treatments on the same area until the skin has fully recovered.

## Food rules that are myths

Many people are told by family to avoid soy sauce, eggs, chicken, seafood or coffee after a laser. It is a common belief in Malaysia, but there is no scientific evidence that dark-coloured food darkens skin or scars. Skin colour comes from melanin your own pigment cells make, not from pigment in your food. A *The Star* fact check in March 2026 reached the same conclusion.

A genuine food allergy is still a reason to avoid that food. Otherwise, a balanced diet helps more than a strict one.

## How long aftercare lasts depends on the laser

There is no single timetable. Non-ablative lasers such as [Pico Laser](/treatments/pico-laser) usually cause redness that settles within a day or two. Ablative lasers that remove the top layer of skin, such as [fractional CO2](/treatments/resurfacing-laser), need longer and more careful aftercare. If you have [melasma](/concerns/melasma) or [pigmentation](/concerns/pigmentation) that darkens easily, your doctor may ask for stricter and longer sun protection.

Follow the aftercare you are given for your own treatment: it is set for the laser, the settings and your skin.
