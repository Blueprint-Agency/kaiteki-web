## What if ageing starts earlier than you think?

## Introduction

Most people associate ageing with fine lines and sagging in their 40s but in reality, our skin begins losing collagen as early as your mid-20s. In tropical climates like Malaysia, where UV exposure is constant, these changes can appear even sooner and become more noticeable over time.

The good news? Early signs of ageing are not something to fear they are signals. When we recognise them early, we have more control and better outcomes. Small, consistent steps can make a visible difference.

In this guide, we will explore the subtle early signs of ageing and what you can do to manage them effectively.

## Why Ageing Starts Earlier Than You Think

Ageing is not something that suddenly appears one day it is a slow, quiet process that starts earlier than most of us realise.

In fact, collagen the protein that keeps our skin firm, smooth, and bouncy actually peaks in our early 20s. After that, our body produces about 1% less of it every year. It seems like a small change, but over time, it adds up in ways we might not immediately notice.

Then there is the environmental factor. When our skin is constantly exposed to UV rays almost every single day, that exposure speeds up the breakdown of collagen, making early signs of ageing show up sooner than expected.

### There are two main types of ageing:

**Intrinsic ageing:** natural, genetic ageing over time

**Extrinsic ageing:** caused by external factors like UV exposure, pollution, and lifestyle

It is also worth noting that Asian skin tends to develop pigmentation and uneven tone earlier, while wrinkles often appear later which is why ageing does not look the same for everyone.

## Fine Lines and Wrinkles

Fine lines are often the first visible signs of ageing, typically appearing in high-movement areas such as the forehead, between the brows, and around the eyes.

In younger skin, elastin allows it to snap back easily after facial expressions. Over time, as elastin decreases, these lines begin to linger.

### This leads to:

**Dynamic wrinkles:** visible only during movement

**Static wrinkles:** visible even at rest

## Uneven Skin Tone and Dark Spots

Uneven skin tone is one of the earliest and most common signs of ageing in sun-rich environments.

It all comes down to melanin, the pigment that gives our skin its colour and helps protect it from UV damage ([SKINCARE TIPS FOR SUNBURN](/blog/tips-to-slow-down-skin-ageing-with-preventive-aesthetic-treatments) ). When our skin is exposed to sunlight, it produces more melanin as a defence. But over time, that process can become uneven. Instead of distributing smoothly, melanin can start to clump leading to dark spots, patches, and an overall uneven tone. This is why many Malaysians notice pigmentation changes before wrinkles even appear. Not all dark spots are the same.

### Common types include:

**Melasma:** larger, hormone-related patches

**Sun spots (age spots):** from prolonged UV exposure

**Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH):** following acne or irritation

The key to managing all of them? Consistent sunscreen, targeted brightening treatments, and, for deeper pigmentation, professional options like laser therapy can help restore a more even complexion.

## Loss of Facial Volume and Skin Laxity

Loss of firmness is not just about the skin it is also about what is happening beneath it. As we age, the fat pads that give our face its natural softness and structure gradually shift downward. At the same time, declining collagen and elastin mean the skin has less support and does not hold its shape as well as it used to.

**The changes can be subtle at first, but in time , we may notice:**

-   Hollowing of the cheeks
-   Deepening nasolabial folds
-   A drooping jawline

Some people may experience this earlier than others. While genetics definitely plays a role, other factors like significant weight loss or chronic lack of sleep can also accelerate the process.

Early treatments such as skin boosters can improve hydration and skin quality, while other collagen-stimulating treatments ([PLINEST PROFHILO](/blog/profhilo)) can help in restoring and maintaining facial structure and support.

## Dull and Rough Skin Texture

If your skin has started to look a little flat or feel rough to the touch, it could be an early sign that your natural renewal process is slowing down. When we are younger, our skin sheds dead cells efficiently, keeping the surface smooth and radiant. But as we age, this cell turnover rate gradually declines allowing dead skin to build up for longer.

**This leads to:**

-   Dullness
-   Rough texture
-   More visible pores

Our daily lifestsyle habits can make this more noticeable; dehydration, inconsistent exfoliation, and poor sleep all contribute to a tired, lacklustre complexion.

How can we slow down this process ? Gentle exfoliating treatments ([SILKPEEL DERMALINFUSION](/blog/silkpeel-vs-hydrafacial)) help by removing the buildup of dead skin, while hydration-boosting facials and skincare can restore a smoother, healthier glow over time.

## Under-Eye Changes: Dark Circles and Tear Troughs

The under-eye area is often the first place to show signs of ageing simply because the skin here is the thinnest on our face. It is delicate, so even subtle changes easily become apparent over time. A common misconception is that dark circles are always due to sleep deprivation ageing is actually a very big contributing factor.

**Dark circles can result from:**

-   Pigmentation
-   Visible blood vessels
-   Shadows that appear due to loss of volume

You may also notice tear troughs, the hollow groove that forms between the lower eyelid and cheek as fat diminishes and shifts.
