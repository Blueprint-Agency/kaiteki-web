## The honest starting point

Patients ask me this one constantly, usually after reading a clinic page that crowns one machine the winner. So let me say the unglamorous part first. The device matters far less than whether it is the right tool for your particular concern, and who is holding it.

Both are excellent picosecond lasers. Both break pigment and tattoo ink into particles small enough for your body to clear, with less heat than the older generation of lasers. Where they differ is wavelength, and how each behaves on darker, Asian skin.

## How picosecond lasers work

A picosecond laser fires energy in pulses measured in trillionths of a second. Those very fast pulses shatter pigment mostly through a shockwave rather than by burning, so less heat spreads into the surrounding skin.

For Malaysian skin tones that is not a minor detail. Excess heat is what raises the risk of post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, the dark marks that turn up after a treatment has been pushed too hard. I see those marks often enough, usually from somewhere else, and they are harder to fix than the original problem.

Both Discovery Pico and PicoSure sit in this picosecond family, so both are gentler than nanosecond lasers. Our [Pico laser](/treatments/pico-laser) page covers how we use the category generally.

## Discovery Pico: the specs

Discovery Pico is made by Quanta. Its reputation is versatility and raw power. Three native wavelengths, 532nm, 694nm and 1064nm, which lets the doctor match energy to the depth and colour of the pigment in front of them.

The 694nm ruby wavelength is genuinely native rather than converted. That is uncommon, and useful for certain pigment and tattoo colours. Pulse durations sit around 370 to 450 picoseconds, and peak power goes very high, up to roughly 1.8 gigawatts at 1064nm.

In plain terms: a flexible workhorse that handles a wide mix of pigment problems and multicoloured tattoos on one device, with 1064nm available as the deeper, generally lower-risk setting for darker skin.

## PicoSure: the specs

[PicoSure](/technology/picosure) is made by Cynosure. It was one of the first picosecond lasers, and it built its name on a 755nm alexandrite wavelength paired with a Focus lens for skin revitalisation. It was also the first picosecond laser cleared by the FDA for melasma, back in 2018.

Pulse width is around 750 picoseconds. That 755nm wavelength is particularly good on stubborn green and blue tattoo inks, and the Focus lens concentrates energy into tiny zones that stimulate collagen. That is why PicoSure has a reputation for brightening and texture work on top of clearing pigment. Newer PicoSure Pro systems add 532nm and 1064nm.

## The melasma reality check

This is the section an honest clinic will not skip. If your concern is melasma, no picosecond laser is a guaranteed fix. Aggressive settings can make melasma darker, and sometimes that shows up weeks later, not on the day.

Melasma is driven by hormones, heat and sun. It usually needs a combination plan: diligent sunscreen, topical lightening agents, sometimes oral options, with laser used cautiously if at all. I have set that plan out in full in [melasma treatment in Malaysia](/blog/melasma-treatment-malaysia-causes-skincare), including what our climate does to it. Anyone telling you they can zap melasma away in two sessions is overpromising, and you should treat that as information about the clinic rather than about the laser.

## Which is safer for Asian and darker skin?

Malaysian skin tones are often Fitzpatrick IV to V. More melanin means more risk of dark marks if a laser is used too aggressively. The 1064nm wavelength penetrates deeper and is absorbed less by surface melanin, which generally makes it the lower-risk choice on darker skin.

Discovery Pico offers 1064nm natively, and that is a real advantage for depth matching on richer skin tones. PicoSure can absolutely be used on darker skin too, but settings and operator skill matter even more.

Here is the practical takeaway. Ask which wavelength will be used on your skin, and why. If the answer is vague, or the same for every patient, be careful.

## Sessions, downtime and cost in Malaysia

Most pigmentation concerns need a course, not a single visit. Often three to six sessions a few weeks apart. Tattoos can take considerably more.

Downtime is usually minimal. Some pinkness, a warm feeling, and pigment that darkens briefly before flaking away over the following days. That temporary darkening is the process working, not a sign something went wrong.

On price, full-face picosecond sessions in Malaysia commonly run from roughly RM600 to RM2,000 per session, depending on the clinic and the work involved. Packages bring the per-session cost down. Treat those as ballpark figures and confirm directly. Be careful with very cheap trial offers, which sometimes mean low energy settings and a course that never quite gets anywhere.

## Will my skin look darker afterwards?

A fair worry, and a common one. For most pigment treatments the spots darken first, crust lightly, then flake off within one to two weeks, leaving clearer skin.

What is not expected is pigment that keeps spreading or darkening past that window. That can signal post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation and should be reviewed. Strict sun protection afterwards is not optional if you want to avoid it. Our [pigmentation](/concerns/pigmentation) page explains how we approach the concern overall.

## So which one gives better results?

Neither is universally better, and any page that crowns one is selling you something.

For a wide mix of pigment types and multicoloured tattoos, Discovery Pico's three native wavelengths and high peak power make it versatile, with a safety edge on darker skin thanks to 1064nm. For green and blue [tattoo ink](/concerns/tattoo-removal), and for brightening and collagen work, PicoSure and its Focus lens are hard to beat.

The real deciding factor is which device fits your concern and your skin tone, in the hands of a doctor who explains their settings rather than just their machine.

## In summary

Choosing between these two comes down to matching the right wavelength to the right problem, on skin that is treated with care. Get clear on your concern first, whether that is freckles, sun spots, melasma, a tattoo or general dullness. Then choose a clinic that talks you through wavelength, settings, and a realistic number of sessions for your skin tone.
